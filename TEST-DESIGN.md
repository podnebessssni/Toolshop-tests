# Checkout payment step — test design

## Scope and environment

This plan covers only the payment step of the guest checkout: submitting a payment decision, showing its outcome, and creating (or not creating) the guest invoice. The API version was confirmed with `GET https://api.practicesoftwaretesting.com/status`, which returned `{"version":"5.0","environment":"production","app_name":"Toolshop"}`.

The checkout payment step was explored against the deployed application using Playwright. The ranking below uses the assignment's verified behavior, the payment-flow contract, and the two payment defects reproduced in the findings section. Earlier checkout steps are outside this plan.

## 2a. Ranked risks

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | How I'd test it | Automate now? | Why / why not |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. A decline is shown as success or creates an invoice. | Critical: the shopper may believe an order was accepted or receive an unintended order. | Medium: payment outcome handling crosses a third-party decision boundary. | Fulfill `POST /payment/check` with `402` and `{ "error": "Financing declined" }`; check status, body, visible message, and no invoice request. | Yes — Part 1 `declined`. | This is the core negative financing path and is explicitly required. |
| 2. Approval is shown but no invoice is created or identified. | High: the shopper has no order confirmation or tracking reference. | Medium: invoice creation is a separate request after approval. | Fulfill approval; check the decision response, success message, one invoice request, and visible invoice number. | Yes — Part 1 `approved`; the live app currently fails this check. | This is the primary customer path and separates payment approval from order creation. |
| 3. A decision service error leaves an ambiguous state or creates an invoice. | High: the shopper cannot tell whether retrying is safe. | Medium: service failures are expected at an integration boundary. | Fulfill `500` with `{ "error": "Payment service unavailable" }`; check exact response, matching UI message, hidden invoice number, and no invoice request. | Yes — Part 1 `serverError`. | A generic error banner alone would not prove the right failure was handled safely. |
| 4. A slow decision allows duplicate submission or gives no pending feedback. | High: duplicate decisions/orders or uncertainty about whether to retry. | Medium: latency can occur while the service is healthy. | Delay the mocked decision; inspect pending UI, submit control state, request count, then the eventual result. | Yes — Part 1 `slowDecision`; it currently finds Confirm remains enabled. | The pending-state check is an explicit assignment requirement; the failing assertion documents a real defect. Do not send a second live submit because it could create a real order. |
| 5. The selected financing method or instalment term is not carried through. | High: the shopper may receive different financing terms than selected. | Medium: method and term are separate UI inputs and request fields. | Select BNPL with 3, 6, 9, and 12 instalments in turn; inspect the payment request and displayed outcome. | Next — add a focused parameterised flow after the required outcomes. | Part 1 currently selects 3 instalments; expanding the matrix is useful after core branching is covered. |
| 6. An unexpected status or response body is treated as approval. | High: contract drift can lead to an unintended order. | Low to medium: API shape changes are possible but not specified in this assignment. | Try an unexpected 2xx body and a non-2xx body without `error`; check safe UI handling and absence of invoice creation. | Next — first clarify the fallback contract with the API owner. | I would not lock in an invented fallback expectation before confirming the contract. |
| 7. Approval succeeds but invoice creation fails or times out. | High: payment may be approved without a usable order confirmation. | Low to medium: invoice creation is a separate downstream operation. | Stub approval, then fail or time out `POST /invoices/guest`; inspect user messaging and retry behavior. | Next — after the expected recovery/idempotency behavior is agreed. | The assignment does not define recovery semantics, so a test now would guess at expected behavior. |
| 8. Payment controls or outcomes are inaccessible by keyboard or assistive technology. | Medium to high: some shoppers may not complete payment or understand its result. | Medium: dynamic errors and progress states can miss focus or announcement behavior. | Manually use keyboard and a screen reader through the payment step; check focus, labels, and announcement of pending/error/success states. | Manual now; automate stable accessibility checks later. | Manual review is better for focus and announcements; add automation once the expected accessible behavior is agreed. |
| 9. Refresh or Back during a pending decision creates duplicate work or hides the outcome. | High: the shopper may retry an unresolved decision. | Low to medium: depends on navigation behavior and idempotency. | Manually explore refresh/Back during the delay; inspect recovery and whether another decision or invoice request occurs. | Manual exploratory check only for now. | The assignment gives no navigation/recovery contract; establish actual behavior before automating it. |
| 10. Real card, bank, or gift-card data is mishandled. | Critical if real payment data is exposed or charged. | Not assessed here: this assignment uses BNPL/bank transfer without entering payment numbers. | Do not enter real PAN, bank, or gift-card details or attempt a live transaction in this assignment. | No — deliberately not tested at all here. | Real payment processing is outside scope and unnecessary for testing the mocked decision contract; no credentials or payment data belong in this suite. |

## 2b. Findings from exploring

I confirmed the target API version with `GET /status`; it returned `5.0`. While checking the payment step, I found two issues:

- I submitted Confirm with a successful payment decision. The page showed “Payment was successful,” but it never displayed an invoice number and sent no `POST /invoices/guest` request. I expected approval to create an invoice and show its reference so the shopper can confirm and track the order. **High severity:** success is shown without an order being created. This is covered by Part 1's `approved` test, which fails on both the invoice request and number assertions.

- I submitted Confirm with a 2.5 second delayed decision and watched the button while the response was pending. Confirm stayed enabled. I expected it to be disabled until the decision returned, so a shopper cannot submit again while the first outcome is unknown. **High severity:** repeated submissions may produce duplicate decisions or orders. Part 1's `slowDecision` test checks the pending button state and fails against the deployed UI.

- Before reaching Payment, I entered `some@email` in the guest email field and the form let me continue. I expected the form to reject an address that cannot receive order updates and explain the problem at the field. **Medium severity:** the shopper may not receive their confirmation or follow-up. This is not covered by Part 1 and is outside the payment-step risk ranking; confirm the product's email-format rule when reporting it.
